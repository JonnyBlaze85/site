How do I write an invoice as a freelance nail tech?

Updated 2026-10-22

Whether you rent a booth, work mobile, or pick up freelance jobs, a clear invoice keeps you and your client on the same page. It shows what you did, what it costs, and when it's due. Most late payments start with a misunderstanding about one of those three things.

This is general information, not legal or tax advice. For advice about your situation, talk to a qualified tax professional or attorney.

What to put on every invoice

Keep it simple. Include these parts every time:

  • Your name or business name, plus a phone number and email
  • Your client's name and contact info
  • An invoice number and the date
  • Each service, the date it happened, and the price
  • Any product or add-on you sold
  • The total due, and any tax you collect
  • The payment due date
  • The ways you accept payment
  • Your payment terms and any late payment wording

Using the same layout every time makes invoices faster to read and easier to track.

A sample layout you can copy

| Item | Date | Price | |---|---|---| | Full set, gel | 12 Oct | $65 | | Nail art, 4 nails | 12 Oct | $20 | | Removal | 12 Oct | $15 |

Subtotal: $100. Tax: as required. Total due: $100. Due: at time of service.

Write payment terms in plain words

"Due at time of service" is easier to understand than industry shorthand. Pick one rule and stick with it. For example: "Due at time of service." or "Due within 7 days of this invoice." Put the rule on the invoice itself, not just in a text message.

Put late fee wording in writing

Nevada law sets a default interest rate when a written contract doesn't fix one, for certain types of debts. That default is the prime rate at the largest Nevada bank plus 2 percent, reset each January 1 and July 1. Any other rate, fee or charge should be written into your agreement. Whether a specific late fee is enforceable depends on the facts, so check with a professional before relying on it.

Keep copies of everything

Save a copy of every invoice, and track what you were paid and when. The IRS guidance for small businesses lists gross receipts as a supporting record and explains that good records help you prepare your tax return. A simple folder or spreadsheet works fine. Ask your tax preparer how long to keep records.

Make sending it easy

Send invoices right after the appointment, not weeks later. Email or text a PDF or a link. Name each file with the client's name and the invoice number.
